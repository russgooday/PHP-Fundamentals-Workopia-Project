Good instinct to want consistency between the two — same shape, same mental model, easier to reason about both at once. On the naming question, there's a real distinction being pointed at here, not just taste.

**What "Services" traditionally means vs. what "Dependencies" means:**

In the pattern you're using, the *container* is the thing that holds dependencies and resolves them — that's just what a DI container *is*, definitionally. So naming your registration class `Dependencies` is, in a sense, naming it after the container's own job description, one level removed. It's not wrong, but it's a little like calling your `Routes` class `Router` — descriptive of the *category* rather than *this specific class's role*.

"Services" comes from a slightly different framing, worth knowing since you'll see it everywhere (Symfony, Laravel's service container, PHP-DI): a **"service"** in this context specifically means *an object that does something for the app* — a Logger, a Mailer, a database connection, your Viewer — as distinct from things like simple config values or entities/models. The container holding them is often literally called the "service container," and the registration file is the "service definitions" or "service registry." So `Services::register()` is really saying "here's where we define the things the app depends on to do work."

**Is that distinction meaningful for you, or pedantic?** Honestly — mostly convention, but conventions matter here for a concrete reason: if you (or anyone else) later reads Symfony docs, PHP-DI docs, or basically any PHP framework tutorial past 2015, "service" is the word that will be used everywhere for this exact concept. If your codebase says `Dependencies`, every time you map tutorial-knowledge onto your own project you're doing a mental find-and-replace. That's a small tax, but it's the kind of thing that adds friction six months from now when you've forgotten this exact conversation.

**That said — "Dependencies" isn't incorrect, and there's an argument for it:** "Services" can feel slightly overloaded/jargon-y to a class holding, say, a `Config` value object or a raw `PDO` connection that isn't obviously a "service" in the active sense. "Dependencies" is arguably more honestly descriptive of *everything* the container might end up holding, service or not. Some ecosystems distinguish this on purpose — a "service container" vs a broader "dependency injection container" aren't always treated as perfect synonyms, even though in practice most PHP frameworks use them interchangeably.

**My actual recommendation:** go with `Services`, mainly for the convention/onboarding reason above rather than because `Dependencies` is wrong — when you go looking for help, examples, or plugins for your framework's pattern in the future, "services" is the term the ecosystem uses, and matching it means less translation work for future-you. But this is genuinely a coin-toss-level naming decision, not a design one — unlike the routes-config question, there's no real structural consequence either way. If `Dependencies` feels more honest to how *you* think about it, that's a legitimate reason to keep it; naming things in a way that matches your own mental model has real value for a solo project.
